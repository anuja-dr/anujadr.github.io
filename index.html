About Me

Understanding Planetary Evolution & Atmospheres

PhD researcher at the University of Vienna investigating non-thermal atmospheric escape, planet formation, and solar-planetary interactions.

I am a PhD student in Astrophysics at the University of Vienna under the supervision of Dr. Kristina Kislyakova. My research investigates non-thermal atmospheric escape processes driven by solar wind and magnetospheric interactions at Earth, Venus, and Mars — crucial mechanisms that govern the evolutionary trajectories and habitability of rocky planetary bodies.

Prior to my doctoral studies, I completed my Master of Science – Bachelor of Science Dual Degree at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Pune, specializing in Physics and Earth & Climate Sciences. I utilize numerical modeling, N-body simulations, and observational spectroscopy to validate theoretical frameworks against empirical data.

First Author Icarus (Elsevier)
2024

Giant planet formation in the Solar System

A. Raorane, R. Brasser, S. Matsumura, T. C. H. Lau, M. H. Lee, A. Bouvier

Icarus, Vol. 421, October 2024, 116231

Investigating the formation and growth pathways of giant planets in the outer Solar System gas disk. Using comprehensive N-body simulations incorporating pebble and planetesimal accretion, we put rigorous dynamical and geochronological constraints on the formation timeline of Jupiter, Saturn, and ice giants.

Master's Thesis IISER Pune
2023

Formation of Saturn and distribution of its growth times

Anuja Raorane (Supervisors: Dr. Ramon Brasser, Dr. Shreyas Managave)

Master's Thesis — Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER), Pune

Running N-body simulations in the outer solar system gas disk and taking a data-based approach from pallasite geochronology to put time-constraints on giant planet formation. Recognised with the Best Master's Thesis Award (IISER Pune) in Earth and Climate Science Department.
