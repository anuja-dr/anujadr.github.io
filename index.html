About Me

Unraveling the Physics of Habitable Worlds

PhD researcher at the Institute of Astrophysics, University of Vienna, investigating exoplanetary atmospheres, climate dynamics, and the physical criteria for planet habitability.

I am a PhD student at the Institute of Astrophysics, University of Vienna. My research centers on understanding planet habitability and the complex atmospheric and climate regimes of terrestrial and sub-Neptune exoplanets.

Through numerical climate simulations (Global Climate Models), radiative-convective equilibrium models, and observational spectroscopy from space observatories (such as JWST and future missions like Ariel), I investigate how stellar radiation environments, atmospheric loss, and chemical compositions influence the long-term habitability of exoplanetary worlds.

Publications & Preprints

Peer-reviewed journal articles, preprints, and research contributions in astrophysics and planetary habitability.

First Author Astronomy & Astrophysics (A&A)
2025

Atmospheric Circulation and Climate Regimes of Terrestrial Exoplanets in the Habitable Zone of M-Dwarfs

Anuja Raorane, M. Güdel, et al.

Astronomy & Astrophysics (A&A), Vol. 690, A42, 2025

Investigating 3D global climate simulations of tidally locked terrestrial exoplanets orbiting active M-dwarfs. We examine how non-synchronous rotation, day-night heat transport, and water vapor cloud radiative feedbacks govern the stability of surface liquid water and define realistic boundaries of planetary habitability.

First Author The Astrophysical Journal (ApJ)
2024

Impact of Stellar XUV Flares and Coronal Mass Ejections on Atmospheric Retention in Habitable Zone Planets

Anuja Raorane, et al.

The Astrophysical Journal (ApJ), 972, 115, 2024

A comprehensive hydrodynamic simulation analyzing the rate of photoevaporation and non-thermal atmospheric erosion induced by repetitive stellar coronal mass ejections (CMEs) and X-ray/EUV radiation bursts. We quantify the critical planetary mass thresholds required for long-term ocean and atmospheric preservation.

High-Resolution Transmission Spectroscopy and Chemical Retrieval of Sub-Neptune and Super-Earth Atmospheres

Collaboration Team, Anuja Raorane, et al.

Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society (MNRAS), 530, 894–908, 2024

Bayesian atmospheric retrievals applied to space-based infrared transit observations, constraining the abundance of carbon dioxide, water vapor, and photochemical hazes across a sample of transiting mini-Neptunes and warm super-Earths.
